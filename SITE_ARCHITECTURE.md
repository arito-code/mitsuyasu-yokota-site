# SITE_ARCHITECTURE.md

## 1. Homepage

### Hero
Goal: Attention + positioning.
- Huge Waglass image
- Kyoto, Japan
- Artist / Architect / Spatial Designer
- Headline: `Preserving Japanese Memory.` or approved alternative
- CTA 1: Available Works
- CTA 2: Private Commission

### Proof strip
Immediately after Hero.
Use four compact proofs:
- Kyoto Station
- Nagoya Castle
- MBS Kyoto Chishin
- International exhibitions

Purpose: prevent “unknown craft seller” perception.

### Waglass
Explain in 3 beats:
1. Authentic Japanese textile
2. Preserved/transformed in glass
3. One-of-one contemporary artwork

### Featured Works
3–6 works maximum.
Do not create a huge catalog on Home.
Each card shows:
- title
- year
- category
- availability
- price or Price on Request
- detail page link

### Kyoto Station
Full-width case study.
Show:
- installation photo
- verified source
- current display status only after final confirmation
- short meaning: “a cultural gateway to Kyoto”

### Artist
Portrait + working image.
Position as:
Artist / Architect / Spatial Designer.
Explain why architecture changes the artwork.

### Calligraphy
Separate artistic pillar.
Potential product:
Personal Kanji Portrait.
Do not present as a spiritual guarantee.
Frame as personalized symbolic portrait.

### In Space
Critical for high-ticket sales.
Show:
- hotel
- residence
- restaurant
- office
- wellness
Future real installation photographs preferred.
CTA: For Interior Designers.

### Journey / Provenance
Kyoto → New York → Austin → etc.
Link to full `/history`.

### Collector Assurance
Four elements:
- One of One
- Certificate / provenance
- Insured worldwide shipping
- Private support / viewing

### Conversion
Three routes:
- Available Works
- Private Commission
- Interior & Hospitality

# Purchase psychology
Sequence:
1. Visual desire
2. Differentiation
3. Authority
4. Story
5. Self-projection
6. Risk removal
7. Ownership
8. Action

Do not put price before adequate context for signature/high-ticket works.
